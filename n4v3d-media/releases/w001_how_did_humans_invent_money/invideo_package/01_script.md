# How Did Humans Invent Money?

Nearly 4,000 years ago, Mesopotamians lent silver at 20 percent interest.

Yet the first coins wouldn't appear for over a thousand years. So how did money begin?

The classic story says people bartered, a goat for some grain. But no one has ever documented a society that ran on barter alone.

Instead, Mesopotamian scribes recorded debts on clay tablets, measured in silver and barley. A shekel was a weight of silver, about eight grams.

So money may have started as a record of who owed what. Historians still debate the details.

Then, in the late six hundreds BCE, in Lydia, in modern Turkey, people stamped lumps of electrum, a natural gold and silver alloy. The first coins.

Today, the Bank of England estimates most money is bank deposits. Numbers in a ledger, a lot like a clay tablet.
