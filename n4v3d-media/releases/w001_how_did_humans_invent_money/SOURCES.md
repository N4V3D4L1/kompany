# Source record — How Did Humans Invent Money?

Author: writer-agent · Fact-checker: research-agent · Expert review: not_required

| Claim | Status | Sources | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** Mesopotamian law codes (c. 1800 BCE, Eshnunna and Hammurabi) set interest on silver loans at 20%. | verified | [R1] | “Mesopotamians made loans of silver or barley at interest rates set by law - under the Ana-Ittisu laws (end of 3rd millennium B.C.), the code of Eshnunna (around 1800 B.C.) and the code of Hammurabi (18th century B.C.) - of 20% for silver and 30% for barley.” |
| **H2** The earliest coins date to the later 7th century BCE — more than a thousand years after c. 1800 BCE. | verified | [R2] | “archaeologists from the British Museum discovered ninety-three electrum coins that had been deposited as offerings during the latter part of the seventh century BCE.” |
| **H3** No pure barter economy has ever been documented ethnographically. | verified | [R3]; [R4] | “No example of a barter economy, pure and simple, has ever been described, let alone the emergence from it of money; all available ethnography suggests that there never has been such a thing.” |
| **H4** Silver and barley served as units of account and media of exchange in Mesopotamia; one shekel ≈ 8.3 g of silver; loans were recorded on clay tablets. | verified | [R1] | “since the 3rd millennium B.C., silver and barley ... were used as medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value ... 1 shekel (= 8.3 grams)” |
| **H5** Money may have originated in credit/accounting rather than as a physical medium; the question is debated. | interpretation (hedged) | [R4]; [R6] | “barter is usually ill-defined and archaeological evidence about it is inconclusive ... non-monetary transactions have persisted during millennia (R4); any story of how money first developed is mostly based on conjecture and logical inference (R6).” |
| **H6** The first coins were made of electrum (natural gold-silver alloy) in Lydia (western Turkey) in the later 7th century BCE. | verified | [R2] | “All earlier coins had been made exclusively of the gold-silver alloy known as electrum ... these earliest coins were inscribed with Lydian names ... minted in Lydia by Lydians. An alloy in which gold occurs naturally” |
| **H7** In 2014 the Bank of England estimated bank deposits make up about 97% of money in circulation in the UK. | verified | [R5] | “[bank deposits] make up the vast majority — 97% of the amount currently in circulation.” |
