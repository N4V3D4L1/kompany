# Source record — What Actually Causes Acne?

Author: writer-agent · Fact-checker: research-agent · Expert review: pending

| Claim | Status | Sources | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** Acne lesions arise in the pilosebaceous unit (the pore: hair follicle + oil gland). | verified | [S2] | “Acne is a prevalent inflammatory dermatological condition that predominantly affects the pilosebaceous unit” |
| **C2** Acne is very common. | verified | [S2] | “Acne is a prevalent inflammatory dermatological condition” |
| **C3** Pores are follicle openings connected to sebaceous (oil) glands that produce sebum. | verified | [S2] | “The pilosebaceous unit is a metabolically active and immunologically responsive niche composed of sebaceous glands, follicular keratinocytes, lipids, proteins...” |
| **C4** Androgens increase sebum production. | verified | [S1]; [S2] | “androgen hormones, specifically testosterone and Insulin Growth hormone (IGH-1), increase sebum synthesis and secretion” |
| **C5** In acne, follicle-lining cells are not shed normally and accumulate with lipids, blocking the follicle. | verified | [S1]; [S2] | “in acne patients, keratinocytes hyper proliferate and are not shed into the lumen, which leads to the accumulation of irregular desquamated corneocytes in the pilosebaceous follicles coupled with lipids” |
| **C6** C. acnes thrives in oily, low-oxygen follicles. | verified | [S1]; [S3] | “anaerobic, lipophilic, gram-positive ... prefers to colonise in sebaceous follicles because they produce large amounts of sebum and provide excellent anaerobic habitat” |
| **C7** C. acnes is the most abundant bacterium on (healthy) skin. | verified | [S3]; [S7] | “C. acnes is, by far, the most abundant bacterium in the skin microbiota, and its load does not seem to differ between healthy skin and skin affected by acne.” |
| **C8** The immune/inflammatory response produces red, swollen (inflammatory) lesions. | verified | [S1]; [S2] | “when the immune system detects P. acnes, the inflammatory process begins ... These mechanistic processes will produce inflammatory lesions such as ... pustules, nodules, cysts and papules” |
| **C9** Acne is not caused by poor hygiene or a dirty face. | verified | [S6] | “Acne is not caused by poor hygiene or having a dirty face (British Association of Dermatologists, Acne Support)” |
| **C10** Acne involves four interacting factors: excess sebum, follicular hyperkeratinization, C. acnes, and inflammation. | verified | [S1]; [S2] | “Acne's pathophysiology involves four important factors: excessive sebum production, hyperkeratinization of pilosebaceous follicles, hyperproliferation of propionibacterium acnes (P. acnes), and inflammation.” |
| **C11** The AAD 2024 guideline strongly recommends benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids. | verified | [S4]; [S5] | “For patients with acne, the AAD recommends benzoyl peroxide — Strong; ... recommends topical retinoids — Strong (moderate certainty). Benzoyl peroxide to reduce the amount of acne-causing bacteria; topical retinoids to help unclog pores and reduce inflammation.” |
| **C12** Painful, scarring or persistent acne warrants seeing a dermatologist (general safety guidance). | verified | [S4] | “Guideline-based management of acne vulgaris is delivered by clinicians; general referral advice, not an individualized recommendation.” |
