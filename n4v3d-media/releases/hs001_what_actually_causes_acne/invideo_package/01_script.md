# What Actually Causes Acne?

Every pimple begins in a place like this. A single pore.

Acne is incredibly common. So what actually goes wrong in there?

Each pore is a hair follicle with an oil gland. The gland makes sebum, and hormones called androgens can push it to make more.

Skin cells lining the follicle should shed and wash out. In acne, they pile up and stick together with the oil, forming a plug.

Inside that plug, a bacterium called Cutibacterium acnes thrives. The surprise? It's the most common bacterium on healthy skin too.

The real trouble is your immune response. Inflammation is what turns a clogged pore into a red, swollen pimple.

So acne isn't caused by dirt. It's oil, clogging, bacteria and inflammation, together. That's why guidelines strongly recommend benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids.

And if your acne is painful, scarring, or not improving, a dermatologist can help.
